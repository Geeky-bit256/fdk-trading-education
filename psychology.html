TRADING PSYCHOLOGY & EMOTIONS

Understand Your Trading Behavior

Recognize the emotions behind your decisions, understand the habits they create, and learn practical ways to respond differently.

Common Trading Behaviors

Explore each behavior to understand what causes it and how you can work toward changing it.

EMOTION 01 FOMO — Fear of Missing Out +

Recognize the behavior

You see the market making a strong move and feel pressure to enter immediately because you think you might miss a profitable opportunity.

Understand the trigger

Fear of missing a move, excitement, or seeing other traders profit can push you to act without waiting for your own trading setup.

What can go wrong?

You may enter late, chase the price, accept a poor entry, or take a trade that does not meet your strategy's requirements.

Practical solution

  • Define your entry conditions before trading.
  • Do not enter simply because the price is moving quickly.
  • If you miss the setup, accept it and wait for another valid opportunity.

Reflect on your behavior

Did I enter because my setup was valid, or because I was afraid of missing the move?

EMOTION 02 Revenge Trading +

Recognize the behavior

After taking a loss, you immediately look for another trade to recover the money. You may increase your risk or enter a setup you would normally avoid.

Understand the trigger

Frustration, anger, and the desire to get back to your starting balance can influence your next decision.

What can go wrong?

You may abandon your strategy, take unnecessary risks, and turn a manageable loss into a much larger one.

Practical solution

  • Set a daily loss limit before trading.
  • Step away from the charts when you feel the urge to recover losses immediately.
  • Only take another trade when your setup meets your predefined rules.

Reflect on your behavior

Would I take this trade if I had not just lost money?

EMOTION 03 Overconfidence After Winning +

Recognize the behavior

After a winning streak or a large profit, you begin believing your next trades are more likely to win. You may increase your position size or ignore your rules.

Understand the trigger

Recent success can make you overestimate your ability to predict the market and underestimate the possibility of another loss.

What can go wrong?

One oversized loss can erase several smaller wins, especially when you abandon your usual risk limits.

Practical solution

  • Keep your risk rules consistent after wins.
  • Evaluate trades by the quality of the decision, not just the profit.
  • Remember that a winning trade does not guarantee the next trade will win.

Reflect on your behavior

Am I following my plan, or do I feel invincible because of my recent results?

EMOTION 04 Fear of Losing and Closing Winners Early +

Recognize the behavior

Your trade moves into profit, but you become afraid that the market will reverse and take those profits away. You close the trade before reaching your planned target.

Understand the trigger

The discomfort of watching an unrealized profit disappear can make you prioritize immediate relief over following your trading plan.

What can go wrong?

You may repeatedly cut winning trades short while allowing losing trades to reach their full stop loss, weakening the risk-to-reward assumptions of your strategy.

Practical solution

  • Define your exit conditions before entering the trade.
  • Use a tested exit plan rather than reacting to every price movement.
  • Review a sample of your trades to see whether early exits actually improve your results.

Reflect on your behavior

Did I close this trade because my plan told me to, or because I was afraid of losing unrealized profit?

BEHAVIOR 05 Babysitting Your Trades +

Recognize the behavior

You constantly watch every price movement, repeatedly check your profit or loss, and feel compelled to interfere with a trade even when your plan has not changed.

Understand the trigger

Anxiety, uncertainty, boredom, or a lack of trust in your strategy can make it difficult to leave a trade alone.

What can go wrong?

You may exit too early, make unnecessary adjustments, or react to ordinary market fluctuations that were already part of your trading plan.

Practical solution

  • Decide in advance how often you need to review an open trade.
  • Set your stop loss and target according to your strategy.
  • Only intervene when a predefined condition requires it, not simply because you feel uncomfortable.

Reflect on your behavior

Has anything changed in my trading setup, or am I watching the trade because I feel anxious?

BEHAVIOR 06 Moving Your Stop Loss to Avoid a Loss +

Recognize the behavior

Price moves against your trade and approaches your stop loss. Instead of accepting the planned loss, you move the stop farther away, hoping the market will reverse.

Understand the trigger

You may be struggling to accept that the trade is not working, hoping to avoid the pain of realizing a loss, or believing that giving the trade more room will save it.

What can go wrong?

Your original risk limit becomes meaningless. If the market continues against you, the eventual loss may be much larger than you planned.

Practical solution

  • Define your invalidation point and stop loss before entering.
  • Do not widen your stop simply to avoid accepting a loss.
  • If your strategy permits stop adjustments, define the exact conditions in advance and never increase risk impulsively.

Reflect on your behavior

Am I adjusting this stop because my trading rules justify it, or because I don't want to take the loss?
