Understanding Risk

Every decision involving uncertainty has possible outcomes. Understanding risk means considering what could go wrong before deciding what to do.

FDK Education 5 min read Beginner

What is risk?

Risk is the possibility that an outcome will differ from what you expected. In financial markets, this can include losing money, experiencing unexpected price movements, or making a decision based on incomplete information.

Risk cannot always be eliminated. However, understanding it can help you make more informed decisions about uncertainty and potential consequences.

Why does risk matter?

Imagine two people considering the same opportunity. One considers only the possible reward, while the other considers both the potential reward and the possible loss.

The second person has considered more information before making a decision. This does not guarantee a better outcome, but it can lead to a more deliberate process.

KEY IDEA

A possible reward tells only part of the story. Understanding the downside is equally important.

A practical example

Suppose a hypothetical trader has R1,000 in an account and considers a trade that could result in a R100 loss.

Account balance R1,000
Potential loss R100
Percentage of account 10%

A R100 loss represents 10% of the hypothetical account. This illustrates why the size of a possible loss matters, not just the amount someone hopes to earn.

This is a simplified example. Actual losses can differ because of price gaps, execution, fees, leverage, and other market conditions.

Questions to consider before a decision

  • What could go wrong?
  • What is the potential downside?
  • What information am I missing?
  • Can I handle the possible consequences?
  • Am I deciding calmly or reacting emotionally?

Final thoughts

Understanding risk does not mean avoiding every uncertain situation. It means recognizing uncertainty, considering possible outcomes, and making decisions with greater awareness.
