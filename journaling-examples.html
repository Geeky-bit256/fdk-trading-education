How I Review My Trades

These examples document how I observe trades, record my reasoning, reflect on my emotions, and identify questions I may want to investigate in future trades. Both winning and losing outcomes are included.

Before You Explore These Examples

IMPORTANT INFORMATION

This page documents my personal trading experiences and learning process. It is not a professional trading demonstration, a recommended trading method, or a set of instructions for other traders to follow.

The screenshots and explanations are not trade signals, invitations to enter or exit trades, or recommendations to buy or sell any financial product. I am sharing what I observed and how I reflect on my own decisions.

My observations are based on my own approach and may not apply to another trader's strategy, circumstances, or risk tolerance.

Trading involves risk, and losses can exceed expectations. You can lose some or all of the money you commit to trading. Nothing on this page guarantees results or prevents losses.

What I Record in My Journal

When reviewing a trade, I want to document more than whether it won or lost. I also want to understand what I observed, what I expected, what actually happened, and how I responded.

  • Market observations: The assets, market data, price behavior, and other information I personally observed.
  • My hypothesis: What I thought might happen and the reasoning behind that expectation.
  • The outcome: What happened after the trade was entered and closed.
  • My emotions: How I felt before entering, during the trade, and after the outcome.
  • Future questions: What I might investigate or test in future trades without assuming that one example proves anything.

References to confirmations or market observations describe what I personally look for. They are not a checklist or entry method for others to copy.

Winning Trade Examples

A profitable outcome is one part of a trade's history. I also want to examine the reasoning behind the decision, whether I followed my intended process, and how I felt throughout the trade.

Winning Trade 1

What I Observed

What did I notice in the market before entering? Which assets, data, or other information did I consider relevant to my own decision?

My Hypothesis

What did I think might happen, and what was the reasoning behind my expectation?

What Actually Happened?

How did the trade develop? Did the outcome match my expectation? Did I follow my plan?

My Emotions

How did I feel before entering, while the trade was open, and after closing it?

What Might I Test Next Time?

Is there a question about my reasoning, execution, or behavior that I want to investigate further?

Winning Trade 2

What I Observed

What did I notice before entering, and what information influenced my thinking?

My Hypothesis

What was I expecting to happen, and why?

What Actually Happened?

What was the outcome? Did I follow my intended process, regardless of the result?

My Emotions

What was I feeling before, during, and after the trade? Did my emotions affect my decisions?

What Might I Test Next Time?

What could I observe across additional comparable trades before drawing a conclusion?

Losing Trade Examples

Losing trades also provide an opportunity to review decisions. Rather than assuming a loss means my reasoning was wrong, I can examine the evidence available at the time, my execution, and whether I followed my intended process.

Losing Trade 1

What I Observed

What did I observe before entering? What information supported my expectation, and what remained uncertain?

My Hypothesis

What did I think might happen, and what was the reasoning behind that expectation?

What Actually Happened?

How did the trade differ from my expectation? Did I follow my plan, and what can I verify from the available evidence?

My Emotions

How did I feel before, during, and after the loss? Did I feel frustrated, fearful, disappointed, or something else?

What Might I Test Next Time?

What question could I investigate further without changing my approach based on one outcome alone?

Losing Trade 2

What I Observed

What did I notice before entering, and what evidence did I use in my own reasoning?

My Hypothesis

What did I expect to happen, and what assumptions was I making?

What Actually Happened?

What was the outcome? Did I follow my plan, or did I make decisions that differed from it?

My Emotions

How did I respond emotionally to the trade? Did those feelings influence any decisions?

What Might I Test Next Time?

What could I investigate across a larger sample of comparable trades before deciding whether a change is justified?

One Trade Is Not Enough to Establish a Pattern

A single winning trade does not prove that a strategy works, and a single losing trade does not prove that it has failed.

When I have a question about my approach, I can record a hypothesis and identify what evidence I would need to examine. I can then review comparable trades over time instead of reacting to every individual result.

Reviewing 20 or more comparable trades can be a useful starting point for an experiment, but that number alone does not guarantee a reliable conclusion. The quality of the records, the consistency of the method, and the limitations of the sample also matter.

What These Examples Are Meant to Show

These examples are a record of my own learning process, not a model that other traders must reproduce. My observations and hypotheses may change as I gather more evidence and review my decisions.

The purpose of keeping a journal is not to justify every trade or find a lesson in every market movement. It is to document what happened, examine my own behavior, and distinguish what I know from what I still need to investigate.
