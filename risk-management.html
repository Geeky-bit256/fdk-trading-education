RISK MANAGEMENT

Protect Your Capital Before Chasing Profits

Losing is part of trading, even when you follow a strategy with a tested edge. Risk management helps you control how much you lose when trades don't go your way, so you can continue trading through difficult periods.

FDK Beginner

Why Risk Management Matters

Every trader experiences losses. A strategy can have an edge over many trades without winning every time. Even when you follow your rules, a trade can still end in a loss.

The problem is not simply losing a trade. The bigger problem is risking so much on individual trades that a losing streak causes serious damage to your account.

You cannot control the outcome of every trade, but you can decide how much of your capital you're willing to risk.

KEY IDEA

Trading isn't just about how much you can make. It's also about how well you manage losses and preserve your ability to continue.

Losing Streaks Are Part of Trading

Imagine following your strategy correctly, only to lose several trades in a row. It can be frustrating, especially when you start questioning whether your strategy still works.

However, a losing streak doesn't automatically mean your strategy has failed. Losses are part of trading probabilities, and even a strategy with a positive expectancy can experience consecutive losses.

This is one reason risk management matters. Your approach should account for the possibility that several trades may lose before another winner appears.

What a Losing Streak Can Look Like

The screenshots below show an example from my own trading experience. Rather than treating each loss as an isolated event, it's useful to examine what a series of losses can look like and why the amount risked on each trade matters.

The lesson isn't that losing streaks can always be avoided. It's that your risk should be planned before you enter a trade, rather than changed in reaction to the outcome of previous trades.

Why Your Risk Per Trade Matters

Consider a hypothetical account with R10,000. If you risk 1% of your starting balance on a trade, your planned risk is R100. At 0.5%, it's R50.

The percentage you choose affects how much capital is exposed when a trade loses. Smaller risk per trade can reduce the impact of a losing streak, although it doesn't eliminate the possibility of substantial losses.

These figures represent planned risk, assuming the trade reaches its stop-loss under normal execution. Actual losses may differ because of slippage, gaps, fees, or other market conditions.

Protect Capital Before Pursuing Profits

It's easy to focus on potential profits when looking at a trading opportunity. But making money is only one side of trading. You must also manage what happens when you're wrong.

Large losses require increasingly larger gains to recover. For example, losing 50% of an account means you need a 100% gain on the remaining balance just to return to where you started.

Risk management is not a guarantee that an account will survive indefinitely. It is a way to control exposure, reduce the damage from losses, and make more deliberate decisions over time.

REMEMBER

You don't need to win every trade. You need a risk plan that accounts for the trades you will lose.

Questions to Ask Yourself

  • How much of my account am I risking on this trade?
  • Can I follow my plan if I experience several losses in a row?
  • Am I increasing my risk to recover previous losses?
  • Have I planned my exit before entering the trade?
  • Am I protecting my capital or chasing profits?
